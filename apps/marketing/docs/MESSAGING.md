# Fourthform messaging

Fourthform designs and builds custom websites for independent businesses. The client portal gives customers one place to share their brief, review the website, approve the launch and manage everyday content.

## Content order

Explain the offer and intended audience first. Keep the first screen to one message and the selected work. Let Home, Work, How we work with you and Pricing provide deliberate routes through the service.

Preserve original portfolio websites, artwork and previews. Clearly distinguish an original website from an archived image or an example interaction. Do not replace an original site with an unrelated reconstruction, invent project names that contradict the artwork, or present the portfolio as commissioned client work without evidence. A selected project can become a design reference in the visitor's brief.

Show the service through concrete actions: understand the business, design and build the website, review the work, then launch and update everyday content. Use the Mori House example to let visitors explore the client portal without committing to a project.

## Product terms

- Direction: input for the website. Initial Direction is the brief and references. During Review, a Direction describes a specific requested change.
- Revision round: one submitted batch of Directions. Saving drafts and sending Initial Direction use no revision round.
- Stages: Direction, Build, Review, Launch.
- Custom website: the public display name for the Site package. The internal key remains SITE.
- One-page website: the public display name for the First package. The internal key remains FIRST.
- Included tools: the public display name for Core, covering content updates, basic analytics, search details and domain management after launch.
- Advanced tools: the public display name for the optional Pro tier, priced at A$39 / month for scheduled content, deeper analytics and search insights.
- State: a scheduled version of selected website content. Explain it as scheduled content before using the product term. Usual content returns when the scheduled period ends.

Shared display labels live in shared/service-labels.ts. Keep database, checkout and saved-draft keys unchanged.

## Scope and pricing

A custom website is A$1,500 for up to 5 custom pages and 3 revision rounds. A$200 starts the project; A$1,300 is due on approval. The one-page website is A$199 for a business opened within the last six months, with around 6 to 7 sections and one revision round. Eligibility is confirmed before work begins. Both include everyday content tools after launch. Additional revision rounds are A$150 each. All prices are in Australian dollars. Domain registration is separate. Shops, booking systems and custom features need an agreed scope and quote.

## Evidence and tone

Use clear words, specific actions and short paragraphs. Explain a product term when it first matters. Keep the editorial headlines, but support them with plain descriptions. Use no em dashes, fabricated testimonials, invented results or claims of awards.

Mori House is an example project. The public portal preview saves edits on the device; account actions, team submissions, payments and publishing in that preview are simulated. Label the example where visitors enter it.

The real website brief starts blank and can be saved on the device or downloaded without an account. Neither action sends the brief to Fourthform. Online submission only appears when the required email service is configured. Keep confirmation messages accurate about what happened. Sample onboarding is an optional preview of the client journey and must not replace the real brief.

The connected client portal is separate from the public example. Only describe an integration as connected or a payment as confirmed when the application has received the corresponding evidence. Do not imply that every production service is provisioned merely because an example control works.

## Decision points

Keep a next action beside each website package. Preserve the chosen package through the real brief. Link scheduled content directly to its focused example. Explain a Direction as a note for the designer before asking a visitor to add one. Let visitors explore independent portal spaces in any order, with a clear return route and no required tour.
